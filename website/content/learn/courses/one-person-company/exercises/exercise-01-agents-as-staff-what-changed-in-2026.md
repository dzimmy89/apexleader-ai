# Exercise 1: Build Your Delegation Map

:::key-concept
You'll turn a week of your real work into a delegation map: every recurring task sorted into "agent alone", "agent drafts, I approve" and "only me", with your first agent job written as a brief. This map feeds every other exercise in the course.
:::

**Time:** 25 minutes · **You'll need:** Any AI chat (free tier is fine), your calendar or to-do list from last week, a doc or spreadsheet · **Builds on:** Lesson 1

## Warm-up

:::checkpoint
Q: Which task belongs in the "agent drafts, I approve" pile?
- [ ] Summarizing three competitor websites for your own notes
- [x] A follow-up email to a client about an overdue invoice
- [ ] Wiring a refund to a client's bank account
Why: The email leaves the business, so you approve it. Research for yourself is safe alone, and money is never delegated.
:::

## Steps

1. **Pick your business.** Use your own. If you do not have one yet, use the sample: *Groomer Books*, a monthly bookkeeping and invoicing service for independent dog groomers, run by one founder.

2. **Dump last week.** Open your calendar, to-do list or sent mail and list every task you did, one per line, with rough minutes. Aim for 20 or more lines. If you use the sample business, ask the AI to generate a realistic week:

```text
Generate a realistic list of 25 tasks a solo founder running "Groomer Books"
(bookkeeping and invoicing for independent dog groomers) does in a week.
Include minutes for each. Mix client work, sales, admin, marketing and finance.
```

3. **Sort it.** Paste your list into an AI chat with this prompt:

```text
Here is my week as a solo founder: [paste list].
Put each task in a table with columns: Task | Minutes | Pile | Reason.
Pile is one of: "Agent alone" (research, summaries, internal drafts, sorting),
"Agent drafts, I approve" (anything sent to customers, posted publicly,
proposals, invoices), or "Only me" (money movement, signing, legal, tax,
hiring decisions). Be strict: if it leaves the business or touches money,
it cannot be "Agent alone". Then total the minutes per pile.
```

4. **Check the sort yourself.** Move any task you disagree with. Pay special attention to anything involving money: payments, refunds and payroll go in "Only me".

5. **Pick your first job.** From the "Agent drafts, I approve" pile, choose the recurring task with the most minutes. Write it as a brief using the template from Lesson 1:

```text
You are my [role] for [business].
Your job: [one recurring job].
You may: read [apps], draft [outputs], save drafts in [place].
You must ask me before: [sends, posts, deletes, setting changes].
You never: move money or share customer data outside these apps.
Each [day/week], report: what you did, what is waiting for me, what you were unsure about.
```

6. **Save the map** as "Delegation Map" in a doc or spreadsheet. You will reuse it in Exercises 2 to 4.

## Done when

- [ ] You have 20 or more real (or sample) tasks with minutes
- [ ] Every task is in one of three piles, and nothing involving money is delegated
- [ ] You know the total minutes in each pile
- [ ] Your first agent job is written as a brief with an approval line
- [ ] The map is saved where you can find it next lesson

:::coached
**For 1-on-1 coaching clients.** Do this on your own business with real data.

1. Pull two weeks of actual calendar and sent-mail history, not one, so you catch fortnightly tasks.
2. Add a column for "cost of a mistake" (low, medium, high) and re-check any high-cost task in the agent piles.
3. Write briefs for your top three delegation candidates, not just one.

**Bring to your session:** your Delegation Map, the three briefs, and the one task you are most nervous about handing off.

**Your coach will:**
- Challenge your pile choices, especially anything customer-facing or financial
- Help you rank the three briefs by hours saved versus risk
- Agree which one you will launch first in Exercise 2
:::
