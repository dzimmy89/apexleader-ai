# Lesson 1: Agents as Staff: What Changed in 2026

:::key-concept
In 2026 AI tools stopped being apps you open and started being staff you assign. The big labs now ship agents that keep working when you close the laptop, check your inbox on a schedule, and come back to you for approval. Your job as founder is to hire them for specific jobs, give them limited access, and decide what they may do without asking.
:::

## What you'll be able to do

- Explain the difference between a chat assistant, a desktop agent and an always-on agent
- Name the main agent products a solo founder can hire in October 2026
- List the jobs in your business and sort them into "agent can do", "agent drafts, I approve" and "only me"
- Use the manager mindset: brief, access, approval rule, review

## Why this matters

A one-person company used to mean one person doing everything, or paying freelancers. Today a founder with a few subscriptions can have a researcher, a bookkeeper's assistant, a scheduler and a junior developer working in parallel. The tools are good enough. What most founders lack is the habit of managing them. If you treat agents like apps, you get random help. If you treat them like staff, you get a team.

## Three kinds of AI help

Think of AI tools in three groups. You will use all three.

| Kind | How it works | Examples (Oct 2026) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chat assistant | You ask, it answers, it stops | Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok chat | Thinking, drafting, research |
| Desktop agent | Works on your computer or files while you watch or step away for a while | Claude Cowork, OpenAI Codex, Cursor | Building files, sites, spreadsheets, code |
| Always-on agent | Runs on its own cloud computer, checks your apps on a schedule, messages you first | Claude Duet, OpenAI dots, Grok Bot | Monitoring, follow-ups, recurring routines |

The always-on group is the new part. Three launches in a few months changed what a solo founder can delegate:

- **Grok Bot** from SpaceXAI entered beta on August 11, 2026. Each Bot has a name, a job, and a cloud computer with a browser, so it can log in to your web apps and work in them. You can run several Bots at once. It is included with paid Cursor plans and SuperGrok plans.
- **OpenAI dots** launched on September 29, 2026. A dot is an always-on agent in ChatGPT with its own cloud computer, access to thousands of apps through plugins, and custom rules that decide what it may do alone. Your first dot is included with ChatGPT Pro or Business Premium.
- **Claude Duet** from Anthropic was announced on October 21, 2026 as a research preview for Pro and Max subscribers in the US, by waitlist. It sends a morning brief, checks your connected apps every few hours, and asks before it sends, pays, signs or deletes anything.

The desktop agents matured too. Claude Cowork works on files in folders you grant it. Codex, OpenAI's desktop agent, writes code, browses, makes images and can use your Mac. Cursor 3 runs several coding agents in parallel. And in June 2026 SpaceX agreed to buy Cursor's parent company, Anysphere, which is why Grok Bot now ships inside Cursor plans.

## The manager mindset

Every agent you hire gets the same four things a new employee would get.

1. **A brief.** One job, written down. "Watch my inbox for client invoices and draft reminders for anything 7 days overdue" is a job. "Help with my business" is not.
2. **Access.** Its own scoped login or connector, only to the apps that job needs. Never your main password.
3. **An approval rule.** What it may do alone, what it drafts for you, and what it never touches.
4. **A review.** A regular look at its work, like a weekly one-on-one.

Here is a brief you can paste into almost any agent to start a job:

```text
You are my operations assistant for [business name], a [one-line description].
Your job: [one specific recurring job].
You may: read [apps], draft [outputs], and save drafts in [place].
You must ask me before: sending anything to a customer, posting publicly,
deleting anything, or changing any setting.
You never: move money, pay bills, or share customer data outside these apps.
Each [day/week], send me a short report: what you did, what is waiting for me,
and anything you were unsure about.
```

## Sort your jobs before you hire

Before you pick tools, list the work. Most founders find their week splits into three piles.

- **Agent can do alone:** research, summaries, sorting, first drafts saved as drafts, internal notes, reminders to you.
- **Agent drafts, you approve:** customer emails, social posts, proposals, invoices, job posts, anything with your name on it.
- **Only you:** moving money, signing contracts, firing a client, tax and legal decisions, anything you'd regret if it went wrong at 3 a.m.

A simple test: if a mistake would leave your business or touch money, a human clicks the final button. That rule holds even when a product technically allows more. Claude Duet, for example, can check out with a one-time virtual card after your approval, and it hard-blocks moving money between accounts. In this course, our house rule is stricter: agents never move money.

:::try-it
Open any AI chat you already use and paste: "Here is a rough list of what I did last week: [paste your calendar or to-do list]. Sort each task into three groups: an AI agent could do it alone, an agent could draft it for my approval, or only I should do it. Explain each choice in five words or fewer." Look at the second group. That is where your first hire will come from.
:::

## What has not changed

Agents in 2026 are capable, but every vendor says the same thing in its own docs: they make mistakes. Anthropic says Duet "might misread a thread or miscalibrate what's urgent." OpenAI tells you to review a dot's consequential work. SpaceXAI tells you to ask a Bot to check the current source before an important decision. Treat every agent like a smart new hire in their first month: useful from day one, checked every day.

Also note what is still early. Claude Duet is a research preview with a waitlist. Grok Bot launched as a beta. Specialist dots for companies are in a pilot preview. Build your business on the durable pattern (brief, access, approval, review), not on any one menu.

:::warning
Never paste passwords, API keys, bank details or customer records into a chat to "help" an agent. Connect apps through the product's own connector or login screen, and keep secrets out of conversations.
:::

:::checkpoint
Q: You want an agent to chase late invoices for your bookkeeping service. Which setup matches the manager mindset?
- [ ] Give it your main email password so it can send reminders as you, any time
- [x] Connect it to your invoicing app with its own access, let it draft reminders, and approve each one before it sends
- [ ] Let it pay the late fees itself so clients are not annoyed
Why: Drafting is safe to delegate. Sending to a customer leaves the business, so it needs your click, and agents never move money.
:::

:::takeaways
- AI now comes in three kinds: chat assistants, desktop agents and always-on agents
- Claude Duet, OpenAI dots and Grok Bot all launched between August and October 2026, and parts are still preview or beta
- Every agent gets a brief, scoped access, an approval rule and a regular review
- Anything that leaves the business or touches money needs a human click
:::
