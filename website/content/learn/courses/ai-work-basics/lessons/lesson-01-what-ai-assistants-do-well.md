# Lesson 1: What AI Assistants Do Well

:::key-concept
An AI assistant predicts useful text from what you give it. That makes it fast at drafting, summarizing, reorganizing and explaining, and unreliable at facts it was never given. Use it where a good first draft saves you time and a human check is cheap.
:::

## What you'll be able to do

- Name four kinds of work AI assistants speed up today.
- Spot the tasks where an assistant is likely to be confidently wrong.
- Pick one task from your own week that is a good first fit.

## Why this matters

Most business owners try AI once on the wrong task, get a wrong answer, and stop. Knowing where it is strong lets you start with a win and build from there.

## Where assistants are strong

Assistants are good at work where the raw material is already in front of them:

- **Drafting**: a first version of an email, a job post, a proposal section.
- **Summarizing**: a long thread, a call transcript, a contract you need the gist of.
- **Reorganizing**: turning notes into a checklist, a table or a step-by-step process.
- **Explaining**: walking you through a spreadsheet formula or a term you do not know.

In each case you can judge the result quickly, because you know what good looks like.

## Where they go wrong

An assistant does not look facts up unless the tool you use says it does. Ask it for a supplier's phone number, a tax rule for your state or last month's sales, and it may produce something that sounds right and is not. Treat any fact, number or quote it gives you as a draft to check.

:::warning
Do not paste customer data, passwords or anything confidential into a tool until you have checked how that tool stores and uses what you type.
:::

## Choosing your first task

A good first task is one you do every week, takes 20 minutes or more, and has an output you can check in two minutes. Weekly customer update emails and meeting summaries are common winners.

:::takeaways
- Assistants draft, summarize, reorganize and explain well.
- They can invent facts; check anything you did not give them.
- Start with a weekly task whose output you can check fast.
:::
