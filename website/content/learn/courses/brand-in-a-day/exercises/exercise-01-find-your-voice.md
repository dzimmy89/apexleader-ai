# Exercise 1: Write Your One-Page Voice Guide

:::key-concept
You'll collect your own writing and your customers' words, have Claude find the pattern, and turn it into a tested one-page voice guide (voice-guide.md). It becomes the first file in the brand kit you finish in Exercise 3.
:::

**Time:** 35 minutes · **You'll need:** a Claude account (free works; a Project is ideal), 3 to 5 samples of your own writing, customer quotes or competitor reviews, your landing page copy if you have it · **Builds on:** Lesson 1

## Warm-up

:::checkpoint
Q: Which is a usable voice trait for an agent?
- [ ] Friendly
- [ ] Best-in-class and customer-centric
- [x] Plain, not dumbed down: short sentences, and any money term explained in five words or fewer
Why: A trait with a contrast and a concrete rule tells an agent exactly what to do and lets you check the result.
:::

## Steps

1. **Set up your workspace.** In Claude, create a Project called "[Business] brand desk" (or use the one from earlier courses). Make a folder on your computer called `brand`.

2. **Gather raw material (10 minutes).** Into one document, paste:
    - 3 to 5 pieces of your own writing you like (emails, posts, a voice memo transcript)
    - 5 to 15 customer quotes about their problem (from interviews, calls, reviews of competitors, community posts). Remove names and contact details.
    - 2 or 3 pieces of writing in your market you dislike

    No business yet? Use Groomer Books and search groomer forums or competitor reviews for real phrasing.

3. **Analyze.** Upload the document to the Project and paste:

    ```text
    Attached: my writing, customer quotes, and writing I dislike. My business: [one sentence].
    1. Describe how I naturally write, quoting examples.
    2. List the exact phrases customers repeat about their problem.
    3. Describe what I dislike in the anti-examples.
    4. Propose 3 or 4 voice traits. For each: the trait, one-line meaning,
       "this, not that," and one example sentence.
    Base everything on the material. Don't flatter me.
    ```

4. **Choose.** Cut any generic trait. Edit the rest until each one would sound odd coming from a competitor. Settle on 3 or 4.

5. **Draft the guide.** Paste:

    ```text
    Write voice-guide.md, under one page, with these sections:
    Who we sound like (one line), Traits (my final traits, each "this, not that"),
    Words we use / words we avoid (pull "use" words from customer quotes),
    Tone by situation (social, sales email, reminders and deadlines, bad news),
    Before and after (three pairs using my real copy),
    Rules for AI writers (American spelling, no em dashes, never invent quotes,
    numbers or prices, mark unknowns [CHECK]).
    ```

    Save it as `brand/voice-guide.md` and upload it to the Project.

6. **Test it.** In a new chat in the Project, paste:

    ```text
    Using voice-guide.md, rewrite: 1) my landing page hero [paste],
    2) an email I sent recently [paste], 3) a 40-word social caption announcing [news].
    After each, list the rules you applied and any you weren't sure how to apply.
    ```

    Read each one aloud. Tighten every rule Claude was unsure about. Run the test once more.

## Done when

- [ ] brand/voice-guide.md exists and fits on one page
- [ ] It has 3 or 4 traits, each written as "this, not that" with an example
- [ ] The "use" word list includes at least five phrases taken from real customers
- [ ] It includes rules for AI writers, including never inventing quotes, numbers or prices
- [ ] A test rewrite of your own copy sounds like you on a good day, read aloud

:::coached
**For 1-on-1 coaching clients.** Pressure-test the voice with real customers and real situations.

1. Write the same message three ways (one per trait pushed to the extreme) and send them to two customers or prospects with one question: "Which sounds most like someone you'd trust with this?"
2. Add a "Hard moments" section to the guide: how you say no, how you apologize for a mistake, how you announce a price rise. Write one real example of each.
3. Have Claude audit your last ten sent emails or posts against the guide and score each, then fix the guide or your habits where they disagree.

**Bring to your session:** voice-guide.md, the customer feedback, the hard-moment examples and the audit results.

**Your coach will:**
- Read the guide aloud with you and cut anything that isn't really you
- Role-play a difficult customer message so you can hear the voice under pressure
- Agree which rules become hard "never" rules for every agent
:::
