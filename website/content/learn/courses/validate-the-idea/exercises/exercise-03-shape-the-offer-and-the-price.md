# Exercise 3: Write the Offer, Set the Price and Ask for a Commitment

:::key-concept
You'll turn your interview evidence into a one-paragraph offer, set a launch price with three anchors, and send a founding-customer ask with a pass bar decided in advance. At the end you'll have Part 3 of your Validated Offer Doc and real commitment data.
:::

**Time:** 35 minutes · **You'll need:** Your Validated Offer Doc (Parts 1 and 2), an AI chat, your interviewees' contact details · **Builds on:** Lesson 3

## Warm-up

:::checkpoint
Q: Which of these is the strongest sign your offer is right?
- [ ] Eight people said they love it
- [ ] An AI rated your offer 9 out of 10
- [x] Three people paid a refundable founding-customer deposit
Why: Commitments, especially money, are real evidence. Praise and AI scores aren't.
:::

## Steps

1. **Draft offer options.** Paste Part 2 into an AI chat and use the offer prompt from Lesson 3 to get three versions. Pick one and edit it until every claim traces back to an interview.
2. **Fill the offer table** in Part 3: who it's for, problem in their words, outcome, what they get, what they do, what it's not, risk reversal.
3. **Price it.** Use the pricing prompt from Lesson 3 with your competitor table (Part 1) and interview data (Part 2). Record:

```text
Floor price (my cost + margin): 
Alternatives range: 
Value estimate (what the outcome is worth to them per month/year): 
Launch price: 
Why this price, in one sentence: 
```

4. **Stress-test it:**

```text
Play a skeptical [customer type] who has heard my offer at [price].
Give me your five toughest objections, in your own words, then tell me which one would actually stop you buying.
```

Add a one-line answer to each objection in Part 3. If you can't answer one, change the offer.
5. **Design tiers** (two or three). For each feature, note which interview it came from. Delete anything without a source.
6. **Set your pass bar** before you ask anyone, and write it in Part 3, for example: "At least 3 of my 8 most interested interviewees pay a refundable deposit or sign a letter of intent within 10 days."
7. **Draft and send the founding-customer ask.** Use the follow-up email prompt from Lesson 3. Edit every email yourself and send it from your own account. Use a payment link you control, with the refund terms and delivery date stated.
8. **Log results** in Part 3 as they come in: who, response, commitment type, objections.

## Done when

- [ ] Part 3 has a one-paragraph offer and a filled offer table tied to interview evidence
- [ ] A launch price with floor, alternatives and value anchors written down
- [ ] Objections answered, and tiers with every feature sourced from an interview
- [ ] A pass bar written before any asks went out
- [ ] Founding-customer asks sent and results logged

:::coached
**For 1-on-1 coaching clients.** Treat it like a real launch:

1. Build a one-page offer sheet (Google Doc or simple landing page) with the offer, tiers, price, refund terms and a payment or booking link.
2. Run two live sales conversations using the offer sheet and note exactly where people hesitate.
3. Model 12 months at your launch price: customers needed to replace your current income, hours per customer, and the point where you'd need help.

**Bring to your session:** Part 3, your offer sheet, notes from the sales calls, and the 12-month model.

**Your coach will:**
- Review your price and tiers against your evidence and your income goal
- Role-play a tough buyer with you
- Help you decide whether you hit your pass bar or need another round
:::
