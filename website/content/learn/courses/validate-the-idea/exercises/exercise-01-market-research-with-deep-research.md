# Exercise 1: Start Your Validated Offer Doc With a Market Scan

:::key-concept
You'll run a Deep Research pass that tries to kill your idea, verify the key numbers, and build a bottom-up market estimate. At the end you'll have Part 1 of your Validated Offer Doc: a competitor table, a market estimate and a ranked list of risky assumptions to test in interviews.
:::

**Time:** 40 minutes · **You'll need:** A deep research tool (Gemini Deep Research works on any Google plan), Google Docs, your business idea or the Groomer Books sample · **Builds on:** Lesson 1

## Warm-up

:::checkpoint
Q: Which research question is most likely to give you an honest picture?
- [ ] "Is there a big market for my idea?"
- [x] "Who already solves this, what do they charge, what do customers complain about, and what would make my idea fail?"
- [ ] "Write me a business plan for my idea."
Why: Asking for competitors, complaints and failure reasons pushes the research to test your idea instead of flattering it.
:::

## Steps

1. **Create the doc.** New Google Doc called "Validated Offer Doc: [idea name]." Add four headings: Part 1 Market, Part 2 Customer Evidence, Part 3 Offer and Price, Part 4 Name and Checks. You'll fill one per exercise.
2. **Write your idea in one line** under the title: "[Offer] for [customer] in [place]." No idea yet? Use "Monthly bookkeeping for independent dog groomers in the US."
3. **Run Deep Research.** In the Gemini app: in the text box click **Add Files**, then **Deep Research**. Paste the "honest case for and against" brief from Lesson 1 with your idea. Click **Submit**.
4. **Edit the plan.** Click **Edit plan**. Cut one step that doesn't serve the decision and add one that does. Click **Start research**.
5. **Verify.** When the report is ready, open the source for each competitor price and each market number. Correct anything that's changed and note the date you checked.
6. **Build the estimate.** In the same chat:

```text
Using only numbers from your report, build a bottom-up estimate of year-one revenue for a solo founder.
One assumption per line with its source: number of potential customers, share that fit, share I can reach in year one, annual price.
Give low, middle and high cases in a table.
```

Copy the table into Part 1 and change any assumption you think is too rosy.
7. **List risky assumptions.** Ask:

```text
List the 8 assumptions this business depends on. For each, rate "how deadly if wrong" and "how much evidence we have" from 1 to 5.
Sort by highest risk first.
```

Paste the list into Part 1. Highlight your top three. These drive Exercise 2.
8. **Write a three-line verdict** in your own words at the top of Part 1: worth testing, why, and the biggest risk.

## Done when

- [ ] Your Validated Offer Doc exists with four part headings
- [ ] Part 1 has a competitor table with prices checked and dated
- [ ] Part 1 has a bottom-up revenue estimate with low, middle and high cases
- [ ] Part 1 lists ranked risky assumptions with the top three highlighted
- [ ] You've written a three-line verdict in your own words

:::coached
**For 1-on-1 coaching clients.** Pressure-test your real idea:

1. Run a second research pass on your strongest competitor alone: their reviews, pricing history and who they ignore.
2. Run the same brief in a second AI research tool and compare the competitor lists. Note anything one tool missed.
3. Write a one-paragraph "why now" argument: what has changed recently that makes this idea more viable.

**Bring to your session:** Part 1 of your Validated Offer Doc, both research reports and your "why now" paragraph.

**Your coach will:**
- Challenge your top three assumptions and your year-one numbers
- Point out competitors or substitutes you haven't considered
- Agree with you on who to interview first in Exercise 2
:::
