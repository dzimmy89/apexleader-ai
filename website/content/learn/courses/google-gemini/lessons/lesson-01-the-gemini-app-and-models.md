# Lesson 1: The Gemini App and Models

:::key-concept
Gemini is Google's AI assistant, and the plan you pay for decides which models you can pick. Use the fast model for everyday work and the stronger model for hard thinking. Give it your business context once, and it becomes a useful junior teammate who already knows your Google account.
:::

## What you'll be able to do

- Choose the right Gemini plan for a one-person business without overpaying
- Pick the right model for a job: Flash-Lite, Flash, Pro, or Gemini 4 Argon
- Set up the app so it knows your business and connects to the apps you use
- Write prompts that get a usable first draft instead of a generic one

## Why this matters

If you already run your business on Gmail, Drive and Google Calendar, Gemini is the AI that sits closest to your work. It can read the email thread you're stuck on, the proposal in your Drive, and the meeting on your calendar. That makes it a strong "staff member" for a solo founder. But in October 2026 Google changed which models each plan gets, so the free version you tried last spring may not be what you get today. Knowing the lineup keeps you from judging the tool on the wrong model, or paying for a tier you don't need.

## The plans in plain terms

Google sells Gemini on two tracks: personal plans tied to a regular Google account, and business plans tied to a Google Workspace or Gemini Enterprise account.

| Plan | What it's good for | Models in the Gemini app (Oct 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Trying it out | Flash-Lite only (Flash and Pro removed for free users from October 9, 2026) |
| Google AI Plus | Light daily use, Daily Brief | Flash-Lite and Flash |
| Google AI Pro | Most solo founders | Flash-Lite, Flash, Pro, and Gemini 4 Argon |
| Google AI Ultra | Heavy agent and video use | Everything, with the highest limits and first access to new features |
| Google Workspace business plans | Your business email and files | Gemini built into Gmail, Docs, Sheets and the Gemini app, with business data protections |

Prices change often, so check the current [Google AI plans page](https://gemini.google/subscriptions/) before you buy. A good default for a founder: Google AI Pro on your personal account while you're exploring, then a Workspace business plan once you have a business domain and customer data. Lesson 5 covers the business plans and Gemini Enterprise in more detail.

Usage is compute-based. Heavier models and longer tasks use more of your allowance, and higher plans have higher limits.

## Pick the right model for the job

Open [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com) or the Gemini app on your phone, Windows PC or Mac. The model menu sits by the prompt box. Here is how to think about each option:

- **Flash-Lite**: quick answers, rewording, simple lookups. Cheap on your allowance.
- **Flash** (currently 3.6 Flash, released July 21, 2026): your everyday workhorse. Summaries, drafts, analyzing a few documents, quick prototypes in Canvas.
- **Pro**: harder reasoning. Pricing decisions, contract comparisons, long documents.
- **Gemini 4 Argon** (in the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra since October 13, 2026): Google's newest frontier model, built for long, multi-step work like analyzing a financial report end to end. Use it when a task has many steps or a wrong answer is expensive.

Google has also said it is adding low, medium and high effort settings for each model. Higher effort means more careful answers and more of your usage limit. If you see the setting, start at medium and go up only when the answer feels thin.

A simple rule: start on Flash. If the answer is shallow or wrong, rerun the same prompt on Pro or Argon and compare. You'll quickly learn which jobs need the bigger model.

## Teach Gemini your business

A generic assistant gives generic answers. Fix that in three places.

**1. Customize.** In October 2026 Google added a Customize option in the left menu of the Gemini app. This is where you set how Gemini responds, add skills (reusable instructions, covered in Lesson 5), and connect apps. Write a short business profile there so every chat starts with it:

```text
I run a one-person business: bookkeeping for independent dog groomers in the US.
My customers are salon owners with 1 to 5 staff who hate paperwork.
I sell a monthly bookkeeping package and a one-time cleanup.
Write in plain American English, short sentences, no hype.
When you're not sure about a fact, say so and tell me what to check.
```

**2. Connect apps.** Gemini can connect to Gmail, Drive, Calendar and, as of October 2026, more than 40 outside apps such as Instacart, OpenTable and Adobe (most are in English in the US). Connect only what you'll use. Every connection is more data Gemini can read.

**3. Attach the real thing.** When you ask about a proposal, attach the proposal. Use the plus button to add a file from your computer or Drive. Gemini answers better from your document than from its memory.

## Prompts that produce usable work

A good prompt names the role, the goal, the input, and the output format. Compare these.

Weak:

```text
Write an email to a customer.
```

Strong:

```text
You're my client-success assistant.
Goal: get a groomer who missed two months of receipts to send them this week, without sounding annoyed.
Context: she's a good client, busy season, pays on time.
Output: a subject line and an email under 120 words, with one clear ask and a deadline of Friday.
Give me two versions: one warm, one more direct.
```

Then refine in the same chat: "Shorter." "Less formal." "Add a line offering a 10-minute call." Gemini keeps the context, so you don't start over.

Two other habits help:

- **Ask for options, then choose.** "Give me three headlines" beats "give me a headline."
- **Ask it to check itself.** "List any claims in this draft I should verify before sending."

:::try-it
Open Gemini, pick Flash, and paste: "Ask me five questions about my business, one at a time, then write a 100-word business profile I can paste into Customize." Answer the questions and save the profile.
:::

## Where Gemini lives

You'll meet Gemini in several places in this course:

- **The Gemini app** (web, phone, Windows with Alt + Space, and Mac): chat, Canvas, image generation, Deep Research and Tasks.
- **Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive** (Lesson 4): help inside the file you're working on.
- **Gemini Notebook, formerly NotebookLM** (Lesson 3): answers grounded only in sources you upload.
- **Skills and Tasks** (Lesson 5): reusable instructions and background agents.

:::warning
Treat any AI chat as a semi-public space. Don't paste passwords, full card numbers, bank logins or customer social security numbers. On a personal Google account, review your activity settings in the Gemini privacy hub before you upload customer files. For client data, a Workspace business account is the safer home.
:::

:::checkpoint

- [ ] Assume Gemini can't do pricing and switch tools for good
- [x] Recognize free users only get Flash-Lite now, and test the same prompt on Pro or Argon with a paid plan or trial
- [ ] Rewrite the prompt in all caps so it takes the task more seriously
Why: Since October 2026 free users get only Flash-Lite. Judge Gemini on the model that fits the task before deciding it can't help.
:::

:::takeaways
- Your plan decides your models: free is Flash-Lite only, AI Pro adds Pro and Gemini 4 Argon.
- Start on Flash, move up to Pro or Argon when the work is hard or high stakes.
- Put a business profile in Customize and attach real files so answers fit your business.
- Keep secrets and sensitive customer data out of chats.
:::
