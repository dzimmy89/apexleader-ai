# Exercise 2: Produce a Cited Competitor Brief

:::key-concept
You'll run a Deep Research project on your market, edit its plan, check its sources and export it. At the end you'll have a "Competitor Brief" Google Doc with a verified competitor table and a list of open questions for customers.
:::

**Time:** 35 minutes · **You'll need:** The Gemini app, your Business Profile Doc from Exercise 1, Google Drive · **Builds on:** Lesson 2

## Warm-up

:::checkpoint
Q: What makes a Deep Research request produce a decision-ready report?
- [ ] Keeping it to one short question so Gemini has freedom
- [x] Naming the decision, the sub-questions, a date range and the output format
- [ ] Asking for the longest report possible
Why: A brief with a decision and a defined output shape keeps the research focused on what you'll actually do next.
:::

## Steps

1. **Open your Business Profile Doc** so you can copy from it.
2. **Start Deep Research** in the Gemini app (in the text box, click **Add Files**, then **Deep Research**) and paste this brief, filling the brackets (or keep the Groomer Books example):

```text
Research goal: Decide how to position [my offer] for [my customer] in [country].
Decision I'm making: which competitor gap to target and a sensible price range.
What I need:
1. The 5 to 8 most relevant competitors or alternatives (including "do it yourself" options), with name, URL, price, what's included.
2. What customers praise and complain about in reviews, forums and social posts.
3. Any market size or count of potential customers, with source and year.
Constraints: prefer 2025-2026 sources. Flag anything older than 2024.
Output: a one-paragraph answer, a competitor table, the top 3 gaps, risks, and 5 open questions to ask real customers.
```

3. **Edit the plan.** After you click **Submit**, click **Edit plan**. Before clicking **Start research**, cut at least one step you don't need and add one you do (for example, "include one software tool customers use instead of hiring someone").
4. **Run it.** Leave it running. Come back when it's done.
5. **Verify the table.** Open the source for each competitor's price. Update the number if it changed and add a "checked on" date.
6. **Ask for a source audit** in the same chat:

```text
List every number in this report with its source and source date. Mark anything inferred rather than read.
```

7. **Export to Google Docs** (in the Canvas panel, **Share & export**, then **Export to Docs**), rename it "Competitor Brief," and move it next to your Business Profile.
8. **Add a summary at the top** in your own words: the one gap you'd target and why, in three sentences.

## Done when

- [ ] A "Competitor Brief" Doc exists in Drive with a competitor table
- [ ] Every price in the table has been checked at the source with a date
- [ ] The report's weak or old claims are marked
- [ ] Your three-sentence summary names one gap to target
- [ ] You have 5 open questions to ask real customers

:::coached
**For 1-on-1 coaching clients.** Go a level deeper on your own market:

1. Run a second Deep Research pass focused only on the gap you picked: who is already trying to fill it and how.
2. Turn on Drive or Gmail as a source (only if relevant) and ask: "How does what my existing contacts say compare with what the market offers?"
3. Build a one-page positioning statement: for [customer], who [problem], [business] is the [category] that [benefit], unlike [main alternative].

**Bring to your session:** both research reports, your verified competitor table, and your positioning statement.

**Your coach will:**
- Challenge the gap you chose and check whether the evidence supports it
- Spot competitors or substitutes the research missed
- Help you turn your open questions into an interview plan
:::
