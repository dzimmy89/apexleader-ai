# Exercise 10: Publish a Comparison Page and an Original-Data Page

:::key-concept
You'll build two citable pages: an honest comparison for a question buyers ask assistants, and a short page of original data from your own business. At the end you'll have the two formats small businesses get cited for most, both checked cell by cell.
:::

**Time:** 45 minutes · **You'll need:** your question bank and topic map, Claude, vendor pricing and feature pages for the options you compare, a number from your own business you can share, your builder agent · **Builds on:** Lesson 10

## Warm-up

:::checkpoint
Q: You can't find a competitor's price on its website. What goes in that cell?
- [ ] Your best guess, marked "about"
- [ ] The price from an old review article
- [x] "Not stated", with the date you checked
Why: Every cell must be verifiable. A wrong fact about a competitor costs more trust than a gap does.
:::

## Steps

1. **Pick the comparison.** Choose a comparison-stage question from your question bank, such as "X or Y?" or "best X for Y".

2. **Pick the attributes.** List the 4 to 6 things buyers decide on, from your question bank and sales calls.

3. **Gather sources.** Open each option's pricing and feature pages. Copy the relevant text into a doc with each link.

4. **Build the table.** Paste the sources into Claude with the comparison prompt from Lesson 10.

5. **Check every cell.** Compare each cell against the vendor's page yourself. Fix anything wrong. Add the date checked.

6. **Write the page.** Ask Claude to write the page around the table using the citable-page shape from Lesson 10: question H1, answer first, one sub-question per section, verdict by buyer type, author and date. Fill any `[NEED]` markers yourself.

7. **Pick your data.** Choose one number from your business you can share: a before-and-after, a median, a count. Check you have the right to publish it and that no customer can be identified.

8. **Write the data page.** Headline number first, then what it covers (how many customers or cases, which dates), the method in two sentences, a simple table or chart in HTML, and what it means for the reader.

9. **Publish both.** Ask your builder agent to publish both pages with Article markup, link them from your pillar page, and open a pull request. Review and merge, then request indexing.

## Done when

- [ ] Your comparison page is live with a table checked cell by cell and dated
- [ ] It names the best option for each buyer type, honestly
- [ ] Your data page is live with the number, what it covers and the method
- [ ] Both are linked from your pillar page and indexing is requested

:::coached
**For 1-on-1 coaching clients.** Make your data a magnet.

1. Design a 3-question survey for your customers that would produce a number your industry would quote.
2. Draft a one-paragraph note offering the data page to a trade newsletter or podcast (Lesson 11).
3. Ask three assistants your comparison question and note who they cite today.

**Bring to your session:** both pages, the survey draft, the note and the assistant results.

**Your coach will:**
- Check your comparison for fairness and accuracy
- Help you pick the survey question with the most pull
- Plan where to share the data first
:::
