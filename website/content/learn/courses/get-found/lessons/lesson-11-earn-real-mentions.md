:::key-concept
A mention is any page on another site that names your business, ideally with a link and an accurate description. Assistants lean on sources they already trust, so the mentions that count are the ones in places they cite for your topic. Earn them by being useful: answer questions as yourself, share your data, appear on niche podcasts and newsletters, get listed in the directories your buyers use, and ask customers for reviews. Never buy, fake or hide your connection to a mention.
:::

## What you'll be able to do

- Find which outside sources assistants cite for your buyers' questions
- Keep one consistent description of your business everywhere
- Build a 90-day mention plan across five channels
- Pitch a newsletter or podcast with one specific idea

## Why this matters

Your own site can only say so much about itself. Assistants want a second opinion, and so do buyers. For a solo founder, outside mentions are also the only way to be found by people who don't yet know your category exists. The work is slow, but each mention keeps working for years, in search, in AI answers and in referrals.

## Find the sources assistants use

Ask the assistants directly, and let Grok and Claude search the rest:

```text
Act as a buyer looking for [your category] for [your customer type].
Question: [buyer question]. Answer normally and list every source you used,
with links.
```

Run it in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and Copilot for your five most important questions. Then ask Claude to group the sources: review sites, directories, publications, podcasts, forums and competitors. Bing's AI Performance report also shows which of your own pages Copilot cites and the grounding queries behind them.

The sources that come up again and again are your target list.

## Five channels for a one-person company

| Channel | What to do | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Directories and associations | Get listed in the 2 or 3 your buyers actually use, with your one-version description | Once, then check yearly |
| Reviews | Ask every happy customer for an honest review on the platform buyers check (Lesson 4) | Every customer |
| Newsletters and publications | Pitch one specific idea or your original data (Lesson 10) to niche editors | 2 pitches a month |
| Podcasts and YouTube | Offer to answer one question your audience asks, with a story and a number | 1 pitch a month |
| Communities | Answer questions as yourself, with your affiliation stated, following each community's rules | 2 helpful answers a week |

### Communities, done right

Reddit, Facebook groups, Slack and Discord communities and X are where buyers ask unfiltered questions, and assistants do cite them. They're also where fake promotion is caught fastest.

- **Use your real name or a clearly labelled business account.** Say you run the business when you mention it.
- **Read each community's rules.** Many ban self-promotion or limit it to set threads.
- **Answer the question fully first.** Mention your product only if it's genuinely relevant, and not every time.
- **Never use extra accounts, paid posters or AI bots** to post or upvote. Communities ban it, Google's spam policies work against it, and it can break consumer-protection rules on undisclosed endorsements.

## The pitch that gets a yes

Editors and hosts get pitched constantly. A good pitch is short, specific and about their audience:

```text
Draft a pitch to [name], who runs [newsletter or podcast] for [audience].
I run [business] and have [one specific asset: a number, a finding, a story].
Offer one idea their audience would find useful, in 2 sentences, with the
specific number. Mention one recent piece of theirs I actually read: [link].
Under 120 words. No flattery, no attachments, one clear ask.
```

You send it, from your own email, after you've read it and checked the recent piece is real.

:::warning
Agents can draft pitches and community answers, but you send and post them yourself, under your name, after reading them. Never let an agent post in communities, create accounts, or contact editors on its own. One spammy message can get your business banned from the community your buyers trust most.
:::

:::try-it
Ask Perplexity your most important buyer question and open every source it cites. How many of those sites have ever mentioned you? Pick the one where a mention seems most achievable.
:::

:::checkpoint
Q: A forum thread asks which bookkeeping app food truck owners use, and you run one. What's the right move?
- [ ] Post from a second account praising your app
- [ ] Ask friends to upvote a thread that mentions you
- [x] Answer the question fully as yourself, say you run one of the options, and follow the forum's rules
Why: Honest, disclosed participation is what communities allow and what lasts. Fake accounts and vote manipulation get banned and work against you in search.
:::

:::takeaways
- Assistants often cite outside sources, so mentions matter as much as your own pages.
- Find the sources assistants already cite for your buyers' questions, and target those.
- Use five channels: directories, reviews, newsletters, podcasts and communities, with a 90-day plan.
- Be useful and honest: real name, stated affiliation, no fake accounts or paid posts.
:::
